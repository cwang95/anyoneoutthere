The translation of this quote into English is (more or less): It will not be Immortal, this Flame, But it will be Infinite, while it lasts... The quote is from Vinicius de Moraes, the great Brazilian poet. In the Portuguese language the word ``Chama'' or ``Flame'' is a euphemism for a deep and abiding love. It seems to me that this quote is just about the most sensible view I've ever heard on the subject. Vinicius also wrote the original Portuguese lyrics for many of the classic early Bossa Nova songs, to the music of the great Brazilian composer Antonio Carlos Jobim. Most Americans think of those songs as frivolous, because they've only heard the ``pop'' English translations. But if you look at the original lyrics, they are generally deeply poetic, painting complex and emotionally mature landscapes. For example, the Vinicius/Jobim song Garota de Ipanema (Americans know the lyrics as ``Tall and tan and young and lovely, the Girl from Ipanema goes walking...'' from Norman Gimbel's translation) is far more interesting in the original Portuguese.
